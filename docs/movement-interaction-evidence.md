# Movement and contextual interaction: issue E02-01

## Candidate and scope

This is an in-progress working-tree candidate based on commit `e56fa570d88ef369689891f93fe09ddfbd27deea`, not a calibrated or playtested release. The candidate consists of the changes to `level.gd`, `first_level.gd`, `movement_component.gd`, `beetle.gd`, `level_door.gd`, `bonus_portal.gd`, `discovery_glow.tres`, `adventure_level_2.tscn`, the expanded beetle regression script, the new integration scripts and their available UID files, alongside this note. No commit has been created. SHA-256 of the gameplay scripts used for these checks: `level.gd` `bb8d942d6835bc200e0d67781ceae038614b5fe903b9d399b5e4886834114ec9`, `first_level.gd` `2a66a369dce249d96f60fa3fa33d41a8d8aed8a1e8ffb0cd8d3e117f22850250`, `movement_component.gd` `be45d5b802c59e0c0d6f8c6303ca5f02c1c617fa3f659b475f1964e233de3c89`.

Static inspection found coyote time of 0.10 seconds, a 0.12-second jump buffer and up to 20 pixels of corner correction in `movement_component.gd`. These are existing prototype values, not values validated with players. Up is shared by jumping, climbing and interaction by default. At a locked door, the shared interaction/jump key is consumed without jumping; a separate jump key remains usable, including when pressed in the same physics frame as, or while holding, a shared key. Available portals take priority over doors; successful interactions lock movement and suppress the shared jump. Ladder movement takes priority while climbing. A portal outside its activation tolerance does not consume the jump. Following user feedback, contextual key/action text and HUD icons were removed. The portal retains its proximity glow, and doors and portals now use a radial gradient halo that strengthens nearby; the door halo stops when it opens. The cue does not change the interaction controls. Input locks and damage locks prevent door and portal activation; repeated key events cannot open a door.

## Remaining evidence

On 2026-11-08, the user reported completing both levels using a keyboard. They perceived a slight movement improvement but said it could improve further. They identified the blue diamond at (2770, 510) as too close to a platform and spike, the beetle at (5300, 550) as trapped by terrain, the spike at (7520, 550) as too far from its neighbor to jump both comfortably, and the beetle at (7800, 550) as sinking through the ground on a stomp and then hurting the player. The exact playtested build was not recorded and no judgment about prompts was supplied.

The revised candidate moves the diamond to (2680, 510), the first stone beetle to (5460, 550), and the second spike to (7500, 550). A collision exception keeps the player from physically pushing a beetle body into terrain while its contact area continues to classify stomps and damage. `second_level_playtest_regression` checks pickup from an adjacent safe position, a single scripted jump over the spike pair, patrol travel, the collision exception and a stomp at the second beetle. The initial placement check failed with the original diamond location; an initial spike jump launched too late and caused damage, while the recorded passing case launches earlier. These are sampled cases, not proof that all approaches are safe or comfortable. The previously tested scene SHA-256 was `19b47d3f64a151d8d2be2700a1c70ce4ccb362f39ee0b4708d9b0138106f25e1`, the beetle script was `bb84b2c6998eeba33acf09236f338ea5c680cfe56db5d80783799903b7607306`, and the regression script was `d01e0e1f4734ae9a71da2981ef420bdb57a60d71641852aefd8afe237573ac74`.

The user subsequently reported replaying the second level, said beetle collisions are now fine, and found the other reported details improved. They did not specify the exact build or separately evaluate prompts, audio or controller support. Since the prior automated run, the second-level scene was saved again; its current SHA-256 is `b0818ae0dc71e6a9e692bc24e9540a32a8624fe97ef3db87d89f068217134829`. The saved scene also places the later `StoneSpikes4` at (11651, 550); this user-workspace change is preserved. The original automated evidence above is for the earlier scene hash, not a claim that the user's playtest used a particular hash.

The user understood the previous prompts but preferred discovery through attractive visual cues, such as a glow. On the first cue revision they reported no noticeable difference at the portal and an edge animation rather than a glow at the door. The current revision replaces this with radial halos. On 2026-11-08 the user reported trying the glow in game and confirmed it is clearly visible at both the portal and door. They subsequently confirmed they ran this same project after the radial halos were added without changing the files before playing; the candidate manifest below remained unchanged on recheck. This is one player's observation and does not establish that new players will discover the interaction unaided.

Audio balance and physical controller behavior have not been independently reviewed. The editor MCP bridge was connected on the follow-up check; its debug workflow could not locate the engine binary for its parse/run step, so the command-line checks use the explicit Godot executable above. Exact timing boundaries and broader movement feel remain to be evaluated. Controller glyphs, configurable assist mode, and co-op controls are not established by this change. Observe varied players with suitable consent before making broader accessibility or calibration claims. No telemetry is collected by this work.
